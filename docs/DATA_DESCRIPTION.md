# Data description (for the Hack2Skill form)

Background: IBM's synthetic anti-money-laundering transactions (Altman et al., NeurIPS 2023; "HI-Small", 5.08 million payments in the stated 10 days, account ids pseudonymised; licence CDLA-Sharing 1.0). We use the rows inside the stated 10 days unchanged (1,108 rows after that range are dropped). We added 90 pig-butchering rings (victims pay collectors, money passes through mule accounts to a cash-out) and 90 legitimate look-alikes (merchants, payroll, landlords, charities, new customers), written by our own code (`data/rings.py`). Their victims are existing IBM accounts, their fraud accounts are new ids, and their amounts and channels are drawn from the IBM data. **Not real fraud:** the whole data set is synthetic, and the rings, the look-alikes, the account open dates (not used by the model) and every result on them are ours. Evaluation only: the public Elliptic and Elliptic++ Bitcoin data. No personal data.
